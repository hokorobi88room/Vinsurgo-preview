When the Next Move Belongs to Someone Else: 3 Ways to Handle the Wait

Use a simple distinction from Epictetus to see what still needs action from you, what now belongs to another person, and what the final answer can teach you.

Written and edited by vinsurgo editorial team Published 01/09/2026 Updated 01/09/2026 Information checked 29/08/2026 6 min read

This article is for you if

You have already sent, asked, applied, apologised, or submitted, but you keep checking because the final answer now depends partly on someone else.

By the end, you will be able to

You will be able to separate what still needs action from you, finish one useful next step, and use the eventual result as information rather than a verdict on yourself.

We have already done what we can. Why does the phone still feel like unfinished business?

You finish a video interview after work and shut the laptop.

On the MRT home, you check your phone once.

No message.

A few stops later, you check again.

The answer matters, so the silence feels active.

Your mind starts inventing extra work: send another note, replay one awkward answer, guess what the interviewer is thinking.

But a situation can still be unresolved even when there is nothing useful for you to do right now.

In Elizabeth Carter’s English translation of Epictetus’s Enchiridion, section 1 begins:

“Some things are in our control and others not.”

The same passage includes “whatever are our own actions” among the things in our control, while reputation and command are listed among things that are not. You can read the passage in the MIT Internet Classics Archive.

For an ordinary day, that distinction gives us one useful question:

What still needs an action from me, and what now has to be decided somewhere else?

Let’s give waiting a boundary

  • Let’s separate our part from the answer we cannot choose.
  • Let’s pick one useful action that can end on our side.
  • Let’s read the eventual result for information, not as a final score on ourselves.

1. Separate your responsibility from the decision that follows it

Imagine you have finished a second-round interview during a lunch break in Singapore.

You would like the role, but the thought “I need them to choose me” joins your work and their decision into one problem.

Your side includes how you prepared, how clearly you answered, whether you promised any follow-up, and what you can improve next time.

Their side includes who else they meet, what the team needs, when they decide, and whom they finally select.

Epictetus’s distinction is useful because it keeps another person’s action from being mistaken for your own. It does not say the outcome is unimportant; it says you cannot perform the other side of the decision yourself.

Try drawing a simple control line:

Still mine

  • Send the document I promised.
  • Note the question that caught me off guard.
  • Prepare a clearer example for the next suitable interview.

Not mine

  • The final hiring decision.
  • The other candidates.
  • The exact time the reply arrives.
  • The opinion the interviewers form afterwards.

The second side can matter deeply without becoming yours to control.

Picture yourself back at your desk after lunch. You write down one answer you want to improve, close the note, and return to the rest of the day.

Nothing has been decided yet.

But you have stopped treating the employer’s part as more work for you.

2. Choose a next move that can actually end

Now imagine the waiting is personal rather than professional.

You sent a careful apology to a friend before boarding the MRT, and the message still has no reply by the time you reach home.

You may want forgiveness, but “make them forgive me” is not an action you can finish alone.

You can check whether the apology clearly names what you did. You can correct something practical if it can still be corrected, then give the other person room to decide what they need.

After that, your immediate part may be complete even while the relationship is still unsettled.

The same method works after sending work to a client or colleague.

Result I hope for:
They accept my proposal.

What depends on them:
Whether they say yes.

What I can finish:
Check what I promised, send anything genuinely missing, and decide when I will look for a reply again.

That last step creates a next check point. It is simply a later time when new information could give you a real decision to make.

Until then, opening email or chat again does not create a new action.

You can put the phone down, have dinner, take a short walk downstairs, or start something else that deserves your attention.

The answer still matters.

It just does not need to occupy every minute before it exists.

3. Let the answer teach you without letting it judge everything

A day later, a reply reaches you while you are having lunch at a hawker centre.

It says no.

Now separate two questions:

What happened?

And:

What does this result actually tell me about the part that was mine?

A careful proposal can still be rejected. A weak proposal can sometimes be accepted.

So the outcome matters, but it is not a perfect measure of how well you acted.

Use the result for the information it can honestly provide.

If several people misunderstand the same part of your proposal, that wording may need work. If several interviews expose the same missing skill, that repeated pattern may show you what to practise next.

You may still feel disappointed when you read the answer.

Return to the control line and ask whether the new information changes something you can do.

If it does, you now have a new action.

If it does not, there may be nothing more to solve in this situation today.

The result can hurt without becoming a command to keep working on what is no longer yours.

First separate the roles, then finish your side, then learn from the reply

These three parts solve different problems. The control line prevents another person’s choice from becoming part of your workload, the finishable next move gives your effort a real stopping point, and the result gives you information only after it arrives.

The order matters. Without the boundary, you may keep sending messages; without a finishable action, you may keep circling the situation; without looking at the result, you may miss feedback that deserves a change.

The aim is not to stop caring about the answer. It is to care without taking responsibility for a decision that is not yours to make.

Move closer to the person you want to become

  • Let’s take one answer we are waiting for and divide the situation into Still mine and Not mine.
  • Let’s choose one useful action from the first side that can honestly finish today.
  • Let’s decide when we will look for new information again, then return the time in between to the rest of our day.
