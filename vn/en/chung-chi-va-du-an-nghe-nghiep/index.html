A certificate is not a career story: build an explainable project

Show others the problems, choices, flaws, results, and limitations of the way you work.

Written and edited by vinsurgo editorial team Published 8/25/2026 Updated 8/25/2026 Information checked 8/25/2026 3 min read

This article is for you if

You have completed the course but do not have a clear application story.

By the end, you will be able to

A five-minute case study that demonstrates the thought process and limitations.

You may have learned a lot, but the way you work is not yet apparent

The certificate documents completion according to the supplier’s terms. It doesn’t show itself how you handle dirty data, change requests, or failed tests. Without a project, effort remains difficult to read for both the employer and yourself.

Short conclusion

  • Choose an issue that is real enough but safe to share.
  • Save the reasoning behind the decision, not just the resulting image.
  • Use errors and corrections to demonstrate evaluation ability.
  • Clearly state the limits and next steps to try.

1. Choose a situation that resembles a real job

Use public or aggregated data, personal projects, authorized community activities, or non-confidential processes. Problems require users, decisions, and constraints. Don’t post company or customer data just to make the project seem “real.”

Instead of “make a dashboard”, write “help store managers see inventory fluctuations from sample data”. Instead of “use AI,” write “check drafts for accuracy, tone, and approval process.”

In 30 seconds, viewers should understand who is being helped and why the project exists. That clarity makes them focus on your thinking rather than the appearance of the tool.

2. Record your choices, errors, and redirections

Save the problem, initial assumptions, initial version, feedback, changes, and final test. Choose an error that really matters: overlapping data groups, users misinterpreting the chart, or automation missing an exception. Explain how to detect and why to fix it.

Write down what the project has not yet proven. The small sample is not representative of the entire enterprise. The prototype is not yet an operating system. Adapted course work should not be described as entirely self-designed if that is not the case.

The limits of honesty do not weaken the story. It shows you can be realistic, get feedback, and make decisions when things aren’t perfect.

3. Present the results and limit them to five minutes

Use the sequence: situation, goal, action, result, limit, next step. Spending the most time on the choice changed the outcome. Explain in plain language, keeping the technical jargon for the deeper question.

If there is data, clearly state how to calculate it. If it’s just a prototype, it’s properly called a prototype. If there are no real users, please state clearly. Ask someone outside the field to listen and tell what they understand.

You can finish calmly: “Here’s what the test supports, here’s what’s unknown, and here’s the next version.” That’s testable confidence.

Summary: the project shows how you think

Meaning-making matters, process evidence shows judgment, and short stories help others understand. The certificate can stand beside, but not replace, the project.

To get closer to who you want to become

  • Choose a user safety issue and decide.
  • Write goals and limits before starting.
  • Save the initial version with important edits.
  • Delete data that is not allowed to be shared.
  • Practice the story for five minutes.
  • Ask someone outside the field to point out the ambiguous part.
